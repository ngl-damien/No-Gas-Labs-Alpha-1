# Stage B — Response-before-future-target protocol contract

**Status:** Proposed protocol specification. Not an approved human-subject study, deployment authorization, or evidence of anomalous information acquisition.

## 1. The question this stage can test

Can a response irreversibly committed before a target is generated later be scored against that target at a rate exceeding a preregistered chance model, under controls that make ordinary information access, target prediction, selective scoring, and retrospective matching implausible?

A positive result would be an anomaly requiring replication and explanation. It would not, by itself, establish time travel, retrocausality, a mechanism, or a "time crystal."

## 2. Temporal objects must not be conflated

Each trial has distinct times:

- `t_protocol`: protocol and analysis plan frozen.
- `t_response`: response bytes frozen and commitment independently timestamped.
- `t_entropy`: the specified future entropy pulse becomes publicly available.
- `t_target`: target generated deterministically from that pulse and the frozen target pool.
- `t_target_commit`: target commitment independently timestamped.
- `t_reveal`: target and proof released to the blind scoring process.
- `t_score`: scoring record frozen.

Required ordering:

`t_protocol < t_response < t_entropy <= t_target <= t_target_commit < t_reveal <= t_score`

The exact equality/ordering rules and clock semantics must be fixed in the preregistration. The protocol must not infer ordering from a laptop's local clock.

## 3. Commit/reveal construction

### 3.1 Freeze the protocol

Before any trial, publish the protocol bytes and digest, target-pool version and digest, scoring rubric, decoy construction, randomization algorithm, primary outcome, sample-size rationale, exclusions, missing-data rules, stopping rule, and analysis code/version. Pin the exact future randomness-beacon source and pulse identifier or the independently audited entropy source. The participant, session operator, scorer, and target generator must have explicitly separated access.

### 3.2 Commit the response first

The response interface emits exact response bytes plus a trial nonce and protocol digest. The nonce must be generated by an independent coordinator before the response, recorded in the trial registry, and unavailable for participant selection or retry. Canonical encoding is specified once (UTF-8, no normalization after capture). Compute a domain-separated commitment, for example:

`SHA256("NGL-TRU-RESPONSE-v1" || protocolDigest || trialNonce || responseBytes)`

Submit that digest to an independent timestamp authority or append-only transparency service. Preserve the signed timestamp token, service identity, verification chain, and raw response bytes in access-controlled escrow. The participant must receive an immutable receipt. Editing or replacing a response creates a new trial; it never overwrites the original.

### 3.3 Generate the target only after the future entropy pulse

After the response commitment is externally observable, obtain the predeclared future pulse and verify its published proof. Derive the target using a versioned, domain-separated deterministic mapping from pulse output, trial nonce, and frozen target-pool digest. Use rejection sampling (or another preregistered unbiased method) when mapping to a finite target set; do not use modulo reduction without correcting bias.

The target pool must be balanced and audited. Its contents and mapping rules may be public before the trial, but the selected target must remain unpredictable until the future entropy pulse. If the pulse, target, or target-selection seed was knowable before the response commitment, the trial is invalid for the primary analysis.

### 3.4 Commit the target and preserve proof

After target generation, canonicalize the target packet and compute a domain-separated target digest. Independently timestamp the target commitment. Preserve the beacon output/proof, target-pool digest, generator version, derivation inputs, target bytes, commitment, signed timestamp evidence, and custody/access logs. Reveal only after the response is locked and the target commitment is recorded.

### 3.5 Blind scoring

The scorer receives randomized response/target/decoy packets without participant identity, condition, or trial chronology where feasible. Score with the frozen rubric. Preserve raw judge scores, adjudication rules, inter-rater agreement, and all exclusions. No post-reveal target replacement, flexible matching, selective deletion, or outcome switching.

## 4. Required evidence envelope

A trial package should contain:

- `protocolDigest`, `trialId`, `trialNonce`, and target-pool digest;
- exact response bytes and response commitment;
- independent timestamp token for the response commitment;
- named entropy source, pulse ID, raw pulse output, and verifiable proof;
- generator code/version and deterministic target derivation record;
- exact target bytes, target commitment, and independent timestamp token;
- reveal/custody/access log;
- blind scoring packet, judge records, and rubric version;
- deviations, incident reports, and analysis inclusion status.

All byte encodings and hash domains are versioned. Corrections are append-only linked records. Hashes prove byte consistency relative to the supplied inputs; they do not authenticate the actor, validate the clock, prove custody, or establish that a target was inaccessible. Those require independently verifiable signatures, timestamp tokens, service logs, and a threat model.

## 5. Threat model and invalidation conditions

| Threat | Required defense | Invalidation condition |
|---|---|---|
| Response edited after seeing target | Immutable response commitment and timestamp | Commitment cannot be independently verified |
| Target selected before response | Future pulse pinned in advance; pulse output unavailable at response time | Target or seed was available or predictable beforehand |
| Timestamp backdating or clock drift | Independent timestamp authority; documented clock model | Ordering rests only on local timestamps |
| Target substitution | Target digest, signed proof, append-only reveal | Commitment does not match revealed target |
| Operator or participant leakage | Role separation, access logs, independent custody | Any plausible unlogged access path |
| Biased target pool or mapping | Frozen pool, audit, unbiased mapping | Distribution or mapping differs from preregistration |
| Retrospective/flexible scoring | Frozen rubric, blind judges, decoys | Scoring rule changed after reveal |
| Selective stopping or deletion | Trial registry, fixed stopping/exclusion rules | Unregistered exclusions or optional stopping |
| Multiple comparisons | One primary outcome, correction plan | Primary claim selected after inspecting results |

A trial with a broken proof, missing timestamp, access-control incident, protocol deviation affecting blinding, or target-generation failure is marked INVALID/DEVIATION before outcome inspection. Do not silently delete it. Report all registered trials and reasons for invalidation.

## 6. Statistical acceptance contract

Before data collection, preregister:

- one primary endpoint and exact scoring rule;
- the chance distribution implied by target and decoy construction;
- minimum effect of interest and simulation-based power analysis;
- sample size and fixed stopping rule;
- exclusion and missing-data handling;
- one primary frequentist analysis and a sensitivity analysis;
- multiplicity correction for secondary outcomes;
- independent replication threshold and replication site/operator.

Report effect sizes and uncertainty intervals, not only p-values. Report nulls, failures, adverse experiences, and deviations. A successful pilot is exploratory. A claim upgrade requires independent preregistered replication and adversarial audit.

## 7. What this protocol does not prove

- A timestamped hash does not prove a person or computer could not access the target through another channel.
- A future public randomness pulse establishes unpredictability only under the beacon's security and availability assumptions; it does not establish metaphysical backward causation.
- A high score can result from a flawed target pool, leakage, bias, scoring error, or chance. These alternatives must be tested.
- Stage A's synthetic retrieval benchmark is a software test, not evidence for Stage B.
- A positive Stage B result would not validate Monroe Gateway, remote viewing, discrete time crystals, or any physical mechanism without separate experiments.

## 8. Go/no-go gates

Do not run a human trial until independent reviewers can reproduce the commitment and target derivation from raw artifacts, verify the external timestamp and beacon proofs, audit access separation, reproduce scoring, and confirm the preregistration. Obtain all applicable ethics, privacy, safety, and institutional approvals before recruiting anyone. If the available infrastructure cannot independently attest the ordering and custody, keep Stage B at protocol simulation only.
