I have been building AI-assisted systems while repeatedly running into a distinction that became more important than the individual systems:

> **A model's assertion is never sufficient evidence that the state it describes is correct.**

That sounds obvious until software begins acting through models.

A model can say a file exists. A workflow can say a job passed. An agent can say it executed a task. A report can say an artifact was deployed. None of those statements, by themselves, establish the state they describe.

## Observation

Experiment 001 treats the gap between assertion and independently inspectable state as an engineering boundary rather than a writing problem.

The working distinction is simple:

**CLAIM** — an actor says something is true.

**EVIDENCE** — an artifact or observation exists outside that assertion and bears on the claim.

**AUTHORITY** — a rule or principal permits a consequence.

**CONSEQUENCE** — the world or canonical system state actually changes.

Collapsing those categories makes AI-assisted systems easy to demo and difficult to trust.

## Interpretation

The principle is not that models are uniquely dishonest.

Humans, logs, documentation, tests and dashboards can all mislead.

The narrower claim is architectural: a consequential system should not require an actor to certify its own success.

This is closely related to established ideas in provenance, reproducible computation, event sourcing, capability security, audit systems and scientific falsifiability. Independent arrival at the problem is not evidence that No_Gas_Labs invented those fields.

## Hypothesis

A useful AI-assisted institution can permit models to make claims freely while requiring consequential state transitions to depend on evidence and authorization outside those claims.

That hypothesis is falsifiable.

If an agent can promote its own unsupported assertion into canonical fact, the boundary has failed.

## Open question

How much autonomy can be granted to models if the surrounding institution preserves an independent veto over what becomes canonical reality?

Experiment 001 exists to make that question executable rather than rhetorical.
