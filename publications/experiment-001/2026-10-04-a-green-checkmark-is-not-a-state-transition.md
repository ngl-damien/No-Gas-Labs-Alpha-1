## Evidence

A passing workflow supports claims about the behavior actually exercised by that workflow.

It does not automatically establish:

- that every governance condition was tested;
- that the tested candidate was authorized for persistence;
- that persistence occurred;
- that external consequences occurred;
- or that canonical state now reflects the candidate result.

The recovered Experiment 001 formulation is blunt:

> **“The workflows passed” no longer implies “the PR satisfied governance.”**

## Interpretation

CI systems are exceptionally good at becoming accidental authorities because their output is compact.

Green.

Red.

Passed.

Failed.

But those labels summarize predicates chosen by whoever built the workflow. They do not independently determine what the predicates should mean.

A green checkmark can therefore be valid evidence and still be insufficient authority for a state transition.

## Negative result

The recovered execution record does not establish canonical persistence for the tested candidate.

Accordingly, this publication does not claim that the canonical state changed.

## Consequence

Experiment 001 requires a stronger chain:

`claim → required evidence → execution → observation → authorization → persistence → independent readback`

A green check can occupy one part of that chain.

It cannot legitimately impersonate the whole chain.

## Open question

How many AI systems currently treat successful evaluation as implicit permission to promote the evaluated output into consequential state?

The answer is not established here.

The smaller result is.

**A green checkmark is evidence about a test. It is not, by itself, a state transition.**
