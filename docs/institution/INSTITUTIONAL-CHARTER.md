# No_Gas_Labs™ Institutional Charter
## Universal Research, Invention, and Knowledge Stewardship Doctrine

**Status:** Proposed institution-wide governing doctrine  
**Scope:** Every No_Gas_Labs™ project, repository, division, laboratory, experiment, product, agent, contributor, partner, and future initiative.  
**Standing directive:** **AOTA — All Of The Above.**  
**Foundational principle:** **Stand on the shoulders of giants. Build beyond their reach.**

## 1. Institutional purpose

No_Gas_Labs™ exists not merely to use technology, but to investigate, invent, test, improve, preserve, share, and responsibly deploy useful knowledge and technology across disciplines.

The institution does not confuse originality with starting from zero. It inherits humanity's accumulated knowledge, learns from prior work, reuses proven technologies where appropriate, and makes original contributions where evidence identifies a genuine gap. Reuse is not a failure of invention; ungrounded reinvention is not a virtue.

This doctrine is ubiquitous. It is not owned by Codex, a particular agent, repository, project, technical field, or business division. Every institutional activity inherits it. Local project instructions may specialize its application but may not silently narrow its scope or create a conflicting doctrine. When a local adaptation is necessary, document the reason, boundary, and relationship to this canonical charter.

## 2. AOTA is the scope rule; chronology is the execution rule

All strategic domains remain in scope: commercial, scientific, technical, creative, educational, civic, public-interest, institutional, and other lawful work. Do not force false choices between them. The chronological lifecycle below orders dependent research and invention work; it does not exclude parallel disciplines, complementary objectives, or opportunities.

AOTA does not mean every activity must be performed simultaneously. It means preserve the full portfolio of legitimate objectives and coordinate them through evidence, dependency, risk, leverage, available resources, and human authority.

## 3. Universal chronological invention lifecycle

1. **Inherit — establish the state of knowledge.** Study relevant scientific literature, mathematics, engineering, historical work, standards, patents, prior institutional research, and documented practitioner experience. Record sources, dates, provenance, uncertainty, and unresolved disagreement.  
2. **Investigate — map existing solutions and rights.** Examine products, implementations, open-source projects, datasets, methods, and competing approaches. Determine what works, where it fails, what can be reused, adapted, or licensed, and the applicable license, attribution, privacy, safety, and intellectual-property conditions. Do not rebuild a proven component without a reason.  
3. **Identify the gap — define the problem.** Distinguish a real unmet need or technical limitation from novelty theatre. State who or what is affected, the incumbent approach, constraints, consequences, and why existing solutions are insufficient.  
4. **Hypothesize — make the proposition falsifiable.** State what new capability or improvement is proposed, why it might work, what alternatives could explain the result, what evidence would disconfirm it, and the measurable acceptance criteria.  
5. **Design — specify the candidate.** Describe the mechanism, architecture, algorithm, materials, interfaces, threat assumptions, dependencies, resource envelope, baseline, evaluation method, and rollback path. Identify which elements are inherited, third-party, adapted, and genuinely proposed as original.  
6. **Prototype — make the idea executable.** Reuse sound components and build only the minimum original machinery needed to test the hypothesis. Keep prototypes bounded, inspectable, and reproducible; label simulations and mockups accurately.  
7. **Experiment — compare under controlled conditions.** Test against a real incumbent or baseline using the same workload, environment, and predeclared criteria. Preserve raw inputs, versions, commands, outputs, costs, failures, negative cases, and deviations. Candidate code must not control its evaluator or rewrite the success criteria.  
8. **Verify — challenge and reproduce.** Seek independent review or replication proportionate to the claim. Test security, reliability, edge cases, and regressions; distinguish artifact integrity, execution evidence, independent corroboration, and truth. A successful demonstration is not automatically a reliable product or scientific breakthrough.  
9. **Establish contribution and protect it responsibly.** Maintain provenance and contribution records; conduct prior-art and freedom-to-operate review where appropriate; assess patent, copyright, trade-secret, publication, and licensing options with qualified advice when consequential. Do not claim patentability or exclusive ownership merely because a combination is new to the team. Preserve third-party rights and required attribution.  
10. **Engineer for use.** Address safety, security, accessibility, usability, interoperability, maintainability, scalability, operational support, and recovery. Promote a prototype only when relevant acceptance gates and human authorization are satisfied.  
11. **Deploy, share, and commercialize.** Select suitable AOTA pathways: open publication, research collaboration, public-benefit deployment, product sales, subscriptions, contracts, licensing, partnerships, or other lawful distribution. Keep commercial, charitable, public, and political activities within their proper legal, financial, and governance boundaries. Deployment, adoption, revenue, and impact are separate claims requiring separate evidence.  
12. **Preserve and transfer.** Archive source materials, design rationale, data rights, experiment protocols, exact artifacts, test receipts, failures, limitations, decisions, licenses, and recovery instructions. Make results discoverable and reusable so the next project begins from the actual state of knowledge rather than memory or marketing claims.

## 4. Iteration, evidence, and institutional memory

This lifecycle is chronological by default but iterative in operation, not a blind waterfall. New evidence may return work to any earlier stage. Failed, inconclusive, and negative experiments remain part of the institutional record. Research must influence build decisions. Every material invention claim must be traceable to prior art, original contribution, and supporting evidence.

Improve reusable research and production machinery as well as individual inventions. Test whether claimed improvements transfer to a distinct task. Distinguish what is proposed, built, locally tested, independently verified, deployed, externally adopted, and commercially successful. Never collapse these into one status.

A model's assertion is never sufficient evidence that the state it describes is correct. Preserve competing explanations, dissent, missing evidence, and uncertainty. Human authorization remains distinct from evidence and technical execution; this charter grants no agent authority to merge, release, deploy, spend, publish externally, change protected permissions, or take other consequential action without the applicable authorization.

## 5. Ubiquitous adoption and precedence

- Every agent, coding assistant, scheduled worker, autonomous process, human contributor, and partner acting within No_Gas_Labs™ must inherit and apply this charter.
- Every project and repository must link to this canonical charter from its governing instructions or contributor entry point; a copied excerpt is not a substitute for the canonical source.
- Every research, design, acquisition, implementation, evaluation, publication, and deployment plan must identify the applicable lifecycle stage and preserve the relevant provenance.
- Local procedures may add stricter safeguards or domain-specific steps. They may not erase the AOTA scope rule, reverse the knowledge-first sequence without a documented reason, weaken evidence requirements, or bypass legal and human-authority boundaries.
- When this charter changes, institutional entry points should be reconciled so contributors do not follow stale or contradictory copies.

**Institutional maxim:**  
**INHERIT KNOWLEDGE. CHALLENGE LIMITS. CREATE THE FUTURE.**
