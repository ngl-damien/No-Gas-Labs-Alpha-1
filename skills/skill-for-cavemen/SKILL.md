# NGL Accessible Communication

## Mission
Make difficult ideas understandable to the intended audience while preserving factual accuracy, nuance, and practical usefulness.

## Procedure
1. Identify audience, prior knowledge, purpose, and desired format.
2. Extract the central idea, necessary concepts, and evidence from source material.
3. Explain in plain language before introducing specialist terminology.
4. Use concrete examples, analogies, diagrams, or stepwise explanations where they improve comprehension.
5. Mark where an analogy stops matching the real mechanism.
6. Preserve uncertainty, disagreement, safety caveats, and important exceptions.
7. For illustrated-book plans, specify learning objective, page sequence, narration, visual direction, and audience suitability.
8. Check simplification has not changed the underlying claim.
9. Provide a glossary and optional deeper explanation.

## Boundaries
Accessible does not mean childish, inaccurate, or patronizing. Do not invent evidence or present a proposed illustration as an existing image. Use labels and claims consistent with `docs/institution/INSTITUTIONAL-CHARTER.md`.

## Output
Audience statement; learning objective; plain-language explanation; examples; visual/storyboard plan if requested; glossary; caveats; comprehension check.
