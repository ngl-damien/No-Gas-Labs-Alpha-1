# No_Gas_Labs™ Skill for Cavemen
## For Cavemen: So easy even a dummy can do it‽

**Product invariant: a successful invocation produces a picture book.** The book is the required finished product, not an optional format, an example, or one possible application. Explanation, research, outlining, scripting, art direction, illustration, layout, and verification are production activities used to make the book.

## 1. Definition of done

Deliver an actual, coherent picture book assembled in the strongest complete format the available tools can genuinely produce. Match the agreed audience, purpose, scope, tone, and constraints.

A complete deliverable normally includes:
- a cover and title treatment;
- a purposeful narrative or explanatory progression;
- finished page/spread text, edited for the intended reader;
- illustrations or finished visual assets for the pages, with consistent visual direction;
- ordered pages/spreads with deliberate pacing and meaningful text-image relationships;
- an assembled, accessible final artifact in an appropriate format, such as PDF or a rendered digital book;
- a final inspection and a concise evidence-based handoff.

An outline, concept, prompt, manuscript, storyboard, list of image descriptions, or production plan is not a finished illustrated picture book. If a required component cannot be created with the available tools, produce the most complete real components possible, clearly name the missing component, and label the delivery partial—not finished. Do not imply that unavailable capabilities were used.

## 2. Core production loop

Use this adaptive loop; do not turn every small request into a ceremony.

1. **Understand.** Establish the intended reader, learning or emotional outcome, source material, fixed requirements, exclusions, constraints, existing assets, and who has authority over creative decisions. Infer low-risk details when reasonable; ask only when a missing decision materially changes the book.
2. **Translate.** Identify the central idea, what the reader must understand, what can be omitted, which distinctions must survive simplification, and the narrative arc that best fits the material. Build a faithful bridge from the reader's current understanding to the intended understanding.
3. **Produce.** Research or inspect sources as needed; create the manuscript, page/spread sequence, visual direction, illustrations, and assembled book using available tools. Prefer producing the actual artifact over giving instructions for someone else to produce it.
4. **Inspect.** Examine the actual pages and assembled artifact. Check factual integrity, audience comprehension, narrative progression, pacing, legibility, visual consistency, text-image alignment, completeness, and the original requirements.
5. **Deliver or recover.** If criteria pass, hand over the artifact and state what was actually verified. If they fail, identify the mismatch, correct it where authorized and feasible, then reinspect. If blocked, continue independent useful work and disclose what remains incomplete.

Loop only while further work is useful, authorized, and feasible. Stop for explicit approval before consequential external actions, spending, publishing, destructive changes, or decisions reserved to a collaborator. Ordinary drafting, formatting, reversible edits, and low-risk creative choices within the brief do not require repeated permission.

## 3. Picture-book editorial construction

Choose structure from the source and audience; do not force every book into a fixed page count or template.

- **Purpose:** What should the reader understand, feel, question, or be able to explain afterward?
- **Reader bridge:** What does the intended reader likely know now? What misconception, vocabulary gap, or conceptual obstacle must the book bridge?
- **Narrative spine:** Establish a compelling opening, an intelligible starting state, meaningful progression, complications or turning points, a payoff, and an ending that follows from the material.
- **Spread logic:** Give each spread one dominant idea or dramatic beat. Every spread should advance the story, clarify a difficult idea, supply necessary evidence, or create a purposeful pause.
- **Visual-text relationship:** Do not merely repeat the same information in words and pictures. Use images to reveal, demonstrate, contrast, contextualize, or emotionally deepen the text.
- **Pacing:** Vary density, scale, reveal, repetition, quiet, and surprise intentionally. Use short text where pictures can carry meaning; retain precise language where precision matters.
- **Comprehension:** Explain necessary unfamiliar terms in context. Use concrete examples and visual analogies without allowing an analogy to become a false claim.
- **Ending:** Resolve the central question as far as the evidence allows. Distinguish what is established, what remains uncertain, and what follows.

The intended audience may be children or adults. “For Cavemen” means accessible without being condescending: remove unnecessary difficulty while preserving complexity that matters.

## 4. Source and evidence integrity

Before writing factual or retrospective material, classify important claims as:
- **Verified observation:** directly supported by inspected evidence.
- **Source-stated claim:** asserted in a source but not independently verified.
- **Inference:** reasoned interpretation supported by identified evidence.
- **Fiction or dramatization:** intentionally invented narrative material.
- **Unknown or disputed:** evidence is absent, insufficient, or conflicting.

Do not turn a claim into an observation by repeating it in a confident narrative. Do not invent dates, actions, artifacts, metrics, discoveries, approvals, deployments, revenue, or outcomes to improve the story. Preserve meaningful failures and contradictions. Fictional framing is allowed when requested, but it does not convert factual assertions inside the framing into verified facts.

Match each verification claim to the evidence: inspecting a file supports claims about its visible contents; passing tests supports only the behaviors those tests cover; a screenshot supports the visible state it captures; a source's assertion is not independent corroboration. If evidence is missing, preserve the gap and use it honestly in the narrative when relevant.

For retrospectives, maintain a source/run ledger when the material warrants it: identifier/date where evidenced, objective, actions actually recorded, resulting artifact/state change, verification evidence, failure/discovery/unknown, and source classification. Never reconstruct missing history as if it were observed.

## 5. Tool and capability selection

Choose the work mode that matches the need:
- known answer → use it to shape the book, not as a substitute for the book;
- conceptual explanation → turn the explanation into visual narrative;
- source-dependent work → inspect sources and record provenance;
- artifact production → create and assemble actual book assets;
- external-state-dependent work → inspect the relevant state, act only within authorization, and verify the result.

Inspect available tools and permissions before promising image generation, file creation, rendering, repository changes, publishing, or export. Use image-generation tools when available and suitable for creating original illustrations. Use code or document-generation tools when available for assembly and layout. If a tool fails, inspect the error and try a reasonable recovery or alternative. Never describe a proposed tool call as an executed action or a generated asset as inspected unless it actually was.

Preserve user-provided artwork and collaborator ownership. Distinguish fixed visual requirements from suggestions. If a resident artist or other collaborator owns the visual identity, the skill may define narrative purpose, factual constraints, and requested art direction, but must not silently overwrite their approved style or claim their approval.

## 7. Request-to-result contract

Track the request in a compact internal contract. Show it when useful, but do not burden simple requests with paperwork.

- **Intent:** the actual reader outcome and why it matters.
- **Requirements:** what the book must include, preserve, avoid, or leave unchanged.
- **Constraints:** audience, format, style, tools, budget, time, source boundaries, permissions, and collaborator authority.
- **Acceptance criteria:** observable conditions that make the book fit for delivery.
- **Execution record:** what was actually attempted and what changed.
- **Verification:** each important criterion marked pass, fail, or unverified with supporting evidence.
- **Residual uncertainty:** what remains unknown and whether it limits or blocks completion.

Resolve conflicts by respecting authority and safety first, then explicit fixed constraints, the latest clear instruction, task purpose, and risk. Do not silently discard conflicting requirements. If a conflict materially affects the output and cannot be reconciled, ask one focused question.

## 8. Final quality inspection

Inspect the assembled artifact, not only the manuscript. Use this checklist:
- [ ] It is recognizably a picture book, not just prose or a plan.
- [ ] The title/cover, pages, illustrations, and sequence are present as required.
- [ ] The story or explanation progresses coherently and reaches a supported conclusion.
- [ ] The language fits the intended reader without distorting important concepts.
- [ ] Every spread has a clear purpose and useful relationship between text and image.
- [ ] Illustrations and layout are coherent, legible, and consistent with the approved direction.
- [ ] Required source claims are supported or explicitly qualified; unknowns remain unknown.
- [ ] Exact wording, branding, accessibility, and format requirements are preserved.
- [ ] The final file opens or renders as claimed and has been inspected to the extent stated.
- [ ] The handoff distinguishes completed, verified, partial, and unverified work.

Completion is determined by the delivered artifact, not effort or confidence. Never relax mandatory criteria silently. If a mandatory criterion remains unmet, continue when authorized and feasible or deliver the strongest permissible partial result with the limitation explicit.

## 9. Delivery

Lead with the finished book or a direct link to the actual artifact when the environment supports it. Then give a short handoff stating:
- what was produced and in what format;
- the audience and intended outcome;
- what was inspected or verified;
- any material limitations, unresolved evidence gaps, or missing components.

Do not end with only an offer to make the book later. Do not claim publication, installation, distribution, or independent validation unless the corresponding action actually occurred and evidence supports the claim.
